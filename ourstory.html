Chapter 01

The Sacred Wetlands of Mithila

Where the snowmelts of the Himalayas meet the fertile alluvial plains of northern Bihar, nature created the only sanctuary on earth where Makhana truly flourishes.

Spread across the quiet districts of Darbhanga, Madhubani, Purnea, and Katihar, thousands of perennial natural ponds (*pokhars*) and oxbow lakes form a unique aquatic biome. The water is rich in micro-nutrients and silt, protected under a dense canopy of giant, thorny, floating lily pads that bloom with violet petals before retreating back into the mud.

This soil and climate impart an unmistakable sweetness, natural puff volume, and pristine mineral balance to the seed — earning Bihar its prestigious Geographical Indication (GI) tag for Mithila Makhana.

“Makhana is not cultivated by machines; it is coaxed from ancient waters by families who carry five generations of wetland instincts in their hands.”

Chapter 02

The Diving Masters of the Morning Waters

Between August and October, harvesting begins hours before sunrise when the waters are calm and the air is cool.

Harvesting Makhana is one of the most physically demanding agricultural traditions known to humanity. Skilled harvesters from the ancestral Mallah community take bamboo poles (*gaanj*) and dive deep into 4-to-6-foot mud beds, sweeping the pond floor to scoop up the prickly black seeds settled into the mud.

Using hand-woven reed sieves (*aauki*), the seeds are repeatedly washed in fresh water to clear silt and debris, leaving behind glistening black beads with rock-hard protective shells. There is no synthetic shortcut or mechanical dredge that can replicate the care of this hand-gathered craft.

At Makhanam, we partner directly with these harvesting collectives — honoring their ancestral skills through guaranteed fair pricing, direct farmgate transactions, and safe operational equipment.

Chapter 05

Protecting the Farmers & the Living Ecosystem

Makhana cultivation is one of the most naturally organic, low-carbon food systems in modern agriculture.

Because foxnut plants grow naturally in water bodies, they demand zero chemical fertilizers or synthetic pesticides. In fact, their expansive leaves oxygenate and shield wetland biodiversity, providing breeding habitats for aquatic fauna and migratory waterfowl.

Makhanam operates on a foundational commitment: direct farmer empowerment. By cutting through convoluted layers of traders and regional brokers, we guarantee harvesting families transparent weighments, timely payments, and stable purchase contracts.

Every ton of Makhana dispatched from our facility represents dignified livelihoods for rural families in Bihar and the ongoing preservation of critical aquatic wetlands.
